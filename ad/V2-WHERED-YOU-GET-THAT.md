# WHERE'D YOU GET THAT — 30s cinema spot (v2)
**Brand:** Maybe It's True · **Format:** 16:9, 24fps, 30s · **Dialogue:** none

## Why v1 read as AI-generated
Six things were doing it, and five are shot-design problems, not model problems:

| Tell | Cause | Fix in v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Melted faces in the crowd | 60 people = 60 chances to fail | Max 4 people in frame, ever |
| The "what's the drop?" shot | Mouths forming words is the #1 tell | No one speaks. The line is a title card |
| Everything drifted / morphed | 5s per shot; generated motion decays after ~2s | 3s generated, 0.8–1.7s used |
| Broken walking | Full-body gait is unstable | Feet only, or back-of-head |
| Odd hands on the hanger | Fine manipulation = finger errors | Hands do ONE simple motion, framed macro |
| Felt like a slideshow | 6 equal 5s blocks is not ad grammar | 20 cuts, accelerating |

## What Nike actually does
Rhythmic cuts on the beat; fast cuts placed against slower ones for contrast;
tight close-ups on faces and product detail; high-contrast grade; music carrying
the emotion. The product is never explained — it's a consequence of the moment.
Streetwear film (Corteiz, Palace, ALD) adds: grain, handheld, crew-as-subject,
real locations, and title cards instead of dialogue.

Both point the same way: **more shots, shorter, tighter, no talking.** That is
also exactly what generated footage is good at. The genre and the medium agree.

## The story
The hoodie arrives. He puts it on. He goes to his friends. One of them touches
the sleeve and wants it. That's the whole film — demand transmitted between two
people, not manufactured by a queue.

## Shot list — 20 cuts

**ACT 3 · THE CREW (0:14–0:25)** — fastest, 0.8–1.4s
12. Out the front door, back view
13. Trainers on wet pavement
14. Three friends waiting on a wall
15. The dap — two hands, macro
16. Four of them on the wall, one laughing
17. A friend's hand pinches the sleeve
18. The friend's face. Eyebrows up. He clocks it

**ACT 4 · THE TURN (0:25:22–0:30)**
19. White flash → card: MAYBE IT'S TRUE (bold white on black, 4s)

One card, no explaining line. The "I want that too" beat is carried entirely by
shots 17 and 18 — the hand on the sleeve and the look — which is why both got
extra frames. Nike never captions the feeling either.

## Sound (spec — Higgsfield cannot generate music)
Act 1 near-silent, room tone and cardboard. A single low sub enters on shot 6.
Percussion from shot 12, tightening each cut through Act 3. Everything drops on
shot 18. Two seconds of silence, then the cards. Cut the picture to the beat —
that is the Nike move and it is what makes 20 shots feel like one gesture.

## Style block
Disposable 35mm, direct flash, hot blown highlights, fast falloff into black,
**neutral-to-cool balance with green only in the shadows** (v1 washed the whole
frame green), heavy grain, halation, plastic-lens corners, vignetting, crooked
handheld framing, orange date stamp. Deep true black on the garment.
